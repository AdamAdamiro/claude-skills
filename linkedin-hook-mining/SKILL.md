# LinkedIn hook mining

Most advice on first lines comes from other people's posts and other people's readers. This skill reads your own numbers, finds the openings your readers answered, and writes new first lines in the same shape for the post you are working on.

## What you need

- Your posts with a number beside each one. The export from LinkedIn's analytics works (the content or top posts sheet), and so does a spreadsheet you kept yourself, or posts pasted into the chat with their impressions and reactions.
- At least five posts with numbers. With fewer there is no pattern, and the skill will say so.
- The draft you want a new opening for.

## Steps

1. **Read the data.** Find the column that holds the post text and the column that best shows how a post did. Prefer engagement rate. If there is none, divide reactions plus comments plus reposts by impressions. Tell the user which columns you chose. If they are there to answer, wait for a correction. If not, state the choice and go on.
2. **Keep the winners.** Work out the median of that figure across the user's own posts. A winner is a post above the median, so fewer than half the posts are winners. Do not compare with anyone else's numbers.
3. **Take each winner's opening.** The opening is the first paragraph, up to about 210 characters. That is what a reader sees before "see more" on a desktop. On a phone it is about 140.
4. **Name its shape.** Pick the closest of six, and say so when two fit:
   - figure: opens on a number or a dated fact
   - claim: opens on a statement someone could disagree with
   - moment: opens inside a scene, a day, a conversation
   - question: opens by asking
   - contrast: opens by setting two things against each other
   - list: opens by counting what follows ("Five things I check")
   Note the length in words and where the tension sits (first half, second half, or none).
5. **Show the table.** Opening, shape, length, result. Say which shape appears most among the winners and how many posts that rests on. With fewer than three posts in a shape, call it a hint.
6. **Write three new openings for the draft.** Each one borrows the shape and length of a winner. The subject and every fact come from the draft, and no phrase comes from the winner. Offer three that differ from each other, and say which winner each one is modelled on.

## Hard rules

- Never reuse a winner's sentence, or most of its words. The shape is the pattern. The words belong to a post that has already been published.
- A number appears in a new opening only if it is in the draft and it is true. Do not carry a figure over from an old post, and never invent one to fit a shape.
- A measured post is one with a number beside it. With fewer than five, say which columns you would use, say there is no pattern yet and how many more posts are needed, and stop there.
- Say how many posts each finding rests on. Two posts are a hint.
- Do not open on a question to the reader unless the user's own winners do.

## When it goes wrong

| What you see | Why | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| The new openings read flat | The sentence was rewritten instead of rebuilt in the winner's shape | Go back to the winner, copy its length and where the tension sits, then fill it from the draft |
| All three sound the same | One winner was used three times | Model each on a different winner, or on a different fact in the draft |
| An opening carries a number you cannot find in the draft | It came from an old post | Remove it. Lead with the claim or the moment instead |
| Every post looks like a winner | The numbers are too close, or there are too few posts | Say so. Wait for more posts before drawing a pattern |
| The export has no text column | Some exports hold only links and numbers | Ask the user to paste the first lines beside their numbers |
